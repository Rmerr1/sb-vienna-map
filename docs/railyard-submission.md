# Publishing Vienna to the Railyard registry

The registry stores **metadata only** — manifests, gallery images and a pointer
to where your ZIP is actually hosted. You host the map yourself; the registry
tells Railyard where to find it and how to check for updates.

Repo: <https://github.com/Subway-Builder-Modded/registry>

---

## 1. Host the map

Create a public GitHub repo — `<your-github-username>/sb-vienna-map` — and push
this project to it (the scripts and config, not the 20 GB of build
intermediates; there's no need to commit the ZIP either).

Then cut a release:

```bash
gh release create v1.0.0 ~/vienna/dist/VIEN-v1.0.0.zip \
    --title "Vienna v1.0.0" \
    --notes "First release. All 23 districts plus Flughafen Wien-Schwechat."
```

Requirements the validator enforces:

- the repo must exist and be public
- it must have **at least one release**
- the latest release must contain a **`.zip` asset**
- the version must be semver — `v1.0.0` or `1.0.0`

## 2. Screenshots

At least one gallery image is required; published maps typically ship 3–4.
Good coverage for Vienna:

1. Zoomed out, whole map — the Danube cutting across, the Gürtel ring visible
2. Innere Stadt at high zoom — building detail and labels
3. A demand overlay showing the hotspots
4. A built line in operation, ideally something recognisably Viennese

Drag them into the issue form or paste URLs, one per line.

## 3. Open the issue

Go to the registry repo → Issues → New issue → **Publish a New Map**.

Numbers come from `dist/submission_values.json`, written by
`scripts/04_package.py`.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Map ID** | `vienna` — permanent, kebab-case, can't be changed later. No Austrian map exists yet, so the plain slug is free. |
| **City Name** | `Vienna` |
| **Additional Cities** | `Schwechat` (the airport is in its territory) |
| **City Code** | **`VIEN`** — matches `config.json` and the ZIP. The schema is `/^[A-Z0-9]{2,4}$/` (verified in `schemas/src/manifest.ts`), so a 4-character code is valid, and `VIE`, `VIEN` and `WIEN` are all still unregistered. ### Methodology text

Paste something close to this — it's what the reviewers actually read:

> Residential demand from the STATISTIK AUSTRIA register-based population grid
> (Regionalstatistische Rastereinheiten, ETRS89-LAEA mesh), aggregated to ~250 m
> demand points placed at the activity-weighted centroid of their constituent
> cells.
>
> Workplace demand: [if you used the official grid] from the STATISTIK AUSTRIA
> workplace/employee grid at the same mesh resolution. [otherwise] modelled — the
> published Vienna employment total is distributed across OSM office, retail,
> industrial and institutional features using generic European per-type employment
> densities, i.e. a fine building-type dasymetric with non-locally-calibrated
> weights.
>
> Origin-destination flows are not observed. They come from a doubly-constrained
> gravity model (Furness/IPF balancing, exponential distance decay) calibrated so
> the modelled commute distribution matches Vienna's published median commute.
>
> Driving times and distances routed with OSRM over the OSM road network. Map
> geometry built with depot; buildings from Overture.
>
> Special demand hand-curated: Flughafen Wien-Schwechat, 18 university/FH
> campuses, 12 major hospitals, stadiums and venues, and 7 outside-connection
> corridors representing the ~250k daily Umland in-commuters.

Being precise here helps you. The rubric scores workplace data at 50% weight,
residents at 35% and observed O/D at 15%, and it explicitly credits *how* mass
was placed below the measured grain. A mesh-based official source with a
building-type dasymetric scores well; overstating it just gets corrected on
review.

### The rubric has changed — re-verified against the registry 2026-08-17

The registry now carries a **`data_quality`** field alongside `source_quality`, and it is the one that matters. Confirmed by cloning `Subway-Builder-Modded/registry`:

- `source_quality` is now **a frozen, write-once legacy tag**. New listings initialise it from the reviewed tier at creation only; it is never rewritten afterwards.
- `data_quality` resolves a continuous [0, 1] score into **six named tiers** — very high, high, medium, low, very low, absent (**A**–**F**). Very high and high collapse to `high`, medium to `medium`, and low/very low/absent to `low`.
- The reviewed tier **supersedes** the self-reported tag.
- Same three pillars and weights as before: Workplace 0.50, Resident 0.35, O/D 0.15. Each of Workplace and Resident now splits further into a **count** (what was measured, at what granularity) and a **dasymetric** (how that count was placed within the measured grain).
- Granularity ladder, best to worst: **Mesh** → ADM5 → ADM4 → ADM3 → ADM2 → ADM1 → None.

**Where Vienna lands.** Residents are strong: the INSPIRE 100 m register grid is *Mesh* granularity with measured counts, the top rung. Workplace is the weak pillar and it carries half the score — a single published city-wide employment total (ADM3 grain at best) spread by a building-type dasymetric with generic European weights that were never calibrated to Vienna. O/D is absent; the gravity model is a model, not an observation. Expect **medium**, and don't be tempted to argue for high — the rubric is applied by a reviewer, not by you, and overstating just gets corrected.

The registry now also ships **`docs/data-quality-questions.md`**, a nine-question guide covering the questions you'll be required to answer before merge. Its two grounding rules are worth internalising before you start typing:

1. Answer with where the numbers are **published**, not where you **put** them. Your demand points are at 300 m; that is not your data granularity. Your job counts are published city-wide.
2. There is an area-size table — use it rather than guessing at the admin-level names.

Questions 1–3 cover workplace source, smallest reported area, and placement method; 4–6 the same for residents; 7–8 whether the census publishes commute flows (Austria: not in what you used); 9 is free-text methodology.

**Registry context, re-checked 2026-08-17:** 309 published maps (up from 296), still **zero for AT**.

### Self-reported quality tags

- `source_quality` — **`medium-quality`** if the jobs layer came from the OSM
  dasymetric. **`high-quality`** is defensible only if you used the official
  Statistik Austria workplace grid. Note the registry hard-caps OSM-based sources
  at medium.
- `level_of_detail` — **`high-detail`** at ~5,000 points over 415 km². (For
  scale: Zurich ships ~39,700 points, Amsterdam ~370.)

## 4. What happens next

1. CI validates the payload and checks the GitHub repo, release and ZIP asset.
   If it fails, edit the issue and comment `revalidate`.
2. A PR is opened automatically with `maps/vienna/manifest.json` and your gallery.
3. You'll be invited to answer the **data-quality questions**. This is required —
   the map cannot merge until a maintainer confirms your answers.
4. On merge, the index regenerates and Vienna appears in Railyard for everyone.

## 5. Updating later

Don't open a new publish issue. Cut a new release (`v1.1.0`), then use the
**Update Map** issue template — it verifies you own the listing via your GitHub
ID and applies only the fields you changed, preserving the listing's history and
download stats.
